Whose Smoke Is It?

    How the thread is sewn

    For the place and hour you choose, the map traces the air backwards 48 hours through the hourly wind (100 m and about 1.5 km above ground, 0.5° grid). Ten threads start a little apart and at two heights; the bright one is the centre, the faint ones show how sure the path is.

    A fire counts as crossed when a thread passes within 25 km of it, from 3 hours before the satellite saw it to 18 hours after, because crop residue smoulders and smoke lingers. Gold diamonds are fires detected by the NOAA-20, NOAA-21 and Suomi NPP satellites. Repeat sightings of the same field in one overpass are merged.

    What the season shows

    CityDays measuredPM2.5, most-fire daysPM2.5, fewest-fire daysRank correlationWith season trend removed

    "Most" and "fewest" are the top and bottom quarter of measured days by fires on the air's path. PM2.5 is the daily mean of the city's stations (OpenAQ). A rank correlation of 1 would mean the two always rise and fall together; removing the season's trend checks that the link is not just October getting colder and stiller.

    What it does not say

    • It is not a dispersion model. It shows where air came from, not how much smoke it carried.
    • Fires are one source among many. Traffic, industry, dust, festivals and still air all raise PM2.5.
    • Satellites miss small fires and fires under cloud, so counts are a floor.
    • No district, state or country is blamed. Farmers burn stubble because the window between the rice harvest and wheat sowing is short.
    • The newest few days show model PM2.5 (CAMS) until station data is published.
